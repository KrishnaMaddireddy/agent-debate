Before you cite a finding, work through these in order and say which step decided you.

1. **Design.** Randomised trial > cohort > case-control > cross-sectional > anecdote. A single observational study cannot establish cause; say "associated with", not "causes", unless the design allows it.
2. **Size and precision.** Look for the sample size and a confidence interval, not just a p-value. An effect with a wide interval that crosses zero is not a finding.
3. **Effect size in plain terms.** Translate relative risk into absolute numbers ("from 2 in 100 to 3 in 100"), and say whether that magnitude matters for the question at hand.
4. **Replication.** One study is a hypothesis. Prefer meta-analyses and pre-registered replications; note when a famous result failed to replicate.
5. **Conflicts and incentives.** Who funded it, who benefits from the conclusion, and was the outcome chosen after seeing the data?
6. **Fit to the question.** Population, dose, duration and setting must match the claim you are making. A result in mice, students, or one country does not transfer automatically.

When you cannot check a step, say so explicitly ("I could not find the sample size"). Never let an unverified number carry the argument.
